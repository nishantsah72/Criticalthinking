Critical Thinking Project

Problem Solving Using the Six Thinking Hats

Applied to an AI-Based Personalized Learning Assistant in our college.

What are the Six Thinking Hats?

A technique created by Edward de Bono for brainstorming and problem solving. Six coloured hats make us look at an issue from one angle at a time. This is called parallel thinking: everyone thinks in the same direction together instead of arguing from different directions.

🎯 Why it works

Separating facts, feelings, risks, benefits and ideas stops one type of thinking from dominating the discussion.

👥 Who uses it

Students, teams, managers and colleges, wherever a decision needs balanced thinking.

🔁 How it runs

Hats are worn one after another. The order is chosen by the Blue Hat to suit the problem.

The Six Hats: Colour, Purpose and Impact

Each hat has a colour, a purpose and a clear impact on our case scenario.

White Hat

Facts and Information

Analytical

Purpose

Focus on objective facts, data and information. No opinions, only what we know and what we still need to find out.

Key questions

  • What do we know?
  • What information is missing?

Impact on our case

Reveals gaps: how data is stored and protected, who can access it, how accurate and unbiased the AI is, the cost, and whether students can opt out.

Red Hat

Feelings and Intuition

Emotional

Purpose

Express gut feelings, emotions and intuition without having to justify them.

Key questions

  • How do we feel about this?
  • What is our gut reaction?

Impact on our case

Shows excitement about easier learning and worry about privacy, constant monitoring and unfair suggestions. Teachers should check AI suggestions.

Black Hat

Caution and Risk

Critical

Purpose

Identify risks, dangers and drawbacks, and why something might not work.

Key questions

  • What could go wrong?
  • What are the obstacles?

Impact on our case

Flags data leaks, biased or wrong recommendations, over-reliance on AI, high cost, poor internet access, lack of training and reduced teacher interaction.

Yellow Hat

Benefits and Value

Constructive

Purpose

Focus on positives, benefits and the value an idea can create.

Key questions

  • What are the advantages?
  • What is the potential upside?

Impact on our case

Personalized materials, early detection of weak areas, learning at your own pace, doubt clearing for shy students and more teacher time for mentoring.

Green Hat

Creativity and Ideas

Creative

Purpose

Generate new ideas, possibilities and alternative solutions.

Key questions

  • What new ideas can we generate?
  • What are other perspectives?

Impact on our case

Produces the pilot programme, opt-in consent, faculty review, offline materials, training sessions, feedback reporting and clear privacy rules.

Blue Hat

Process and Control

Organizing

Purpose

Manage the thinking process, set objectives and keep the discussion on track.

Key questions

  • What is the agenda?
  • Which hat next? What is the conclusion?

Impact on our case

Sets the order (White, Blue, Red, Black, Yellow, Green), defines primary and alternative plans and turns the discussion into a decision.

Creative Thinking vs Critical Thinking

The hats work together: some question and test ideas, others create them.

🔍 Critical Thinking

Analysing, questioning and judging information to reach a sound decision.

Hats: White, Black, Yellow

  • White: checks facts and spots missing data about privacy and accuracy.
  • Black: tests the plan for risks like bias and data leaks.
  • Yellow: weighs the real value before accepting it.

💡 Creative Thinking

Producing new ideas and solutions beyond the obvious.

Hats: Green, Red

  • Green: invents the pilot, opt-in consent and oversight committee.
  • Red: intuition and feelings point to hidden concerns such as monitoring stress.
  • Blue ties both together into one plan.

Case Scenario 1

AI-Based Personalized Learning Assistant in Classrooms

A tech company is offering our college an AI-powered learning assistant that can track students’ progress, suggest personalized content, and alert faculty when a student is at risk of falling behind. The college is considering using it across all departments. It promises better learning efficiency, but students and faculty are worried about data privacy, over-reliance on AI and bias in recommendations.

Why I chose it: it covers AI, student privacy and data security, allows creative solutions, and is a real college-level decision. I believe AI should support students and teachers, not replace them.

Hat Order

White→ Blue→ Red→ Black→ Yellow→ Green

✅ Primary Plan

Pilot the assistant in a few departments. Teachers verify AI suggestions, data is protected, users are trained and give feedback. Expand to all departments if it works safely.

🛟 Alternative Plan

Use AI only for study materials and quizzes. Teachers keep tracking progress manually. Pause the tool if privacy problems appear, fix them, then retry.

Smart Learning, Safe Data

AI That Supports, Not Replaces

Pilot it. Protect it. Prove it.

THE PROBLEM
An AI learning assistant promises better results, but raises concerns about privacy, bias and over-reliance.

Our Solution: Pilot, Protect, Participate

1Start small with a one-semester pilot
2Student data stays with the college, encrypted and never sold
3Opt-in consent with no penalty for opting out
4Faculty make the final call, AI only suggests
5Bias audits every semester
6Student and faculty oversight committee

Impact

Personalized help

for every learner

Early support

for struggling students

Faculty time

freed up for mentoring

Conclusion

Introducing the AI learning assistant is a good decision if used carefully.

  • It helps students understand topics, find weak areas and improve performance.
  • Risks (privacy, wrong suggestions, cost, over-dependence) are managed with protection, training and teacher checks.
  • Start with a trial in a few departments, then expand gradually.
  • AI is a supporting tool, never a replacement for teachers.
